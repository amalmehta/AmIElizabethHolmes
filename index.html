    The real point

    Fraud is when claims outrun evidence and you hide the gap.

    This test scores what you’d do, not how you feel. Feeling like a fraud is something else, called impostor feelings, first described by Clance & Imes (1978). Worrying that you overclaim is a reason to check your work, not proof that you do.

    A rule you can reuse

    Before you say it, ask: could I show this to someone who wants it to be false? If yes, say it. If not, say what you can show (“three pilot users”, “a recording”) and let it be enough. It works like a unit test for claims, and it fails when you’re the only person checking.
